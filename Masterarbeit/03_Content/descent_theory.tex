\chapter{Descent Theory}\label{chap:descent-theory}

In Chapter~\ref{chap:grothendieck-topologies} we formulated the sheaf condition via the \v{C}ech nerve: a presheaf of sets $F$ satisfies descent along $p:U\to X$ if
\[
F(X)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\lim_{[n]\in\Delta}F\bigl((U/X)_n\bigr),
\]
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:tot-cosimplicial-set} only the truncation $\Delta_{\le 1}$ of the indexing category matters, since $\Set$ is a $1$ category and all higher coherence are automatic. Hence, the condition becomes an equalizer condition on $U$ and $U\times_XU$. In this chapter $F$ takes values in categories and groupoids, the main examples being quasi-coherent sheaves and modules. Two things change. First, pullbacks compose only up to canonical isomorphism, so $F$ is a \emph{pseudofunctor} rather than a functor.
Second, compatibility of local data is no longer an equality on $U\times_XU$ but a specified isomorphism, which in turn has to satisfy a cocycle condition on $U\times_XU\times_XU$.
These changes reflect the $2$-categorical nature of \Cat and \Grpd.

We take this explicit description, an object over $U$, an isomorphism over $U\times_XU$ and a cocycle condition over $U\times_XU\times_XU$, as the definition of the descent category (Definition~\ref{def:classical-descent-data}); it is the description used in all computations below. Its conceptual meaning, the $2$-categorical analogue of the limit above,
\[
F(X)\;\simeq\;\Tot F\bigl((U/X)_\bullet\bigr),
\]
is explained in Section~\ref{sec:descent-totalization} in a sequence of remarks. There we also explain how the classical description arises naturally from the $2$-categorical totalization depending only on $\Delta_{\le 2}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:desc-2-truncation}), which is the $2$-categorical analogue of Lemma~\ref{lem:tot-cosimplicial-set}, and visualize as the following limit diagram:

\subsection*{What the Keel--Mori theorem needs}

We only develop as much descent theory as is used later.
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Representable functors are fpqc sheaves} (Section~\ref{sec:representable-fpqc}). This uses that the structure sheaf satisfies fpqc descent, which is the fully faithful part of descent for quasi-coherent sheaves (Theorem~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-qcoh}).
	\item \textbf{Descent of affine morphisms} (Corollary~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-affine-schemes}). It is used to see that quotient stacks $[\Specof{A}/G]$ by finite groups are stacks (torsors under a finite constant group are finite \etale, in particular affine) and it is the base case of effective descent for quasi-affine and for separated, locally quasi-finite morphisms (\cite[][Tags 0245, 0247, 02W8]{stacks-project}), which enters the proof that the quotient of a scheme by an \etale\ equivalence relation is an algebraic space (\cite[][Proposition~2.2.15, Theorem~3.4.11]{alper2026stacks}).
	\item In the proof of the Keel--Mori theorem itself one only uses \etale\ descent of morphisms and of isomorphisms between algebraic spaces and the theorem on quotients by \etale\ equivalence relations (\cite[][Theorem~4.3.12]{alper2026stacks}). The first two are formal consequences of the fact that algebraic spaces are sheaves for the \etale\ topology; for schemes this is~(1).
\end{enumerate}
Accordingly, the chapter proceeds along the following chain:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small, row sep=small]
	\text{Amitsur complex} \arrow[r, Rightarrow] & \text{faithfully flat descent for modules} \arrow[d, Rightarrow] \\
	\text{descent of affine morphisms} & \text{fpqc descent for }\QCoh \arrow[l, Rightarrow]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
All other descent statements are quoted from \cite{stacks-project} when they are needed.

\begin{definition}[Pseudofunctor]\label{def:pseudofunctor}
	(\cite[][Definition~3.10]{vistoli2007notes})
	Let $\CatC$ be a category. A \emph{pseudofunctor} $F:\CatC^{\op}\to\Cat$ consists of the following data:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item a category $F(T)$ for every $T\in\CatC$;
		\item a functor $g^*:F(T)\to F(T')$ for every morphism $g:T'\to T$, called \emph{pullback along $g$};
		\item a natural isomorphism $\epsilon_T:(\mathrm{id}_T)^*\Rightarrow\mathrm{id}_{F(T)}$ for every $T$;
		\item a natural isomorphism $\alpha_{h,g}:h^*g^*\Rightarrow(g\circ h)^*$ for every pair of composable morphisms $T''\xrightarrow{h}T'\xrightarrow{g}T$.
	\end{enumerate}
	These are subject to the following two coherence conditions.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item[(U)] For $g:T'\to T$ we have $\alpha_{\mathrm{id}_{T'},g}=\epsilon_{T'}g^*$ and $\alpha_{g,\mathrm{id}_T}=g^*\epsilon_T$ as natural transformations $g^*\Rightarrow g^*$ (after the evident identifications of source and target).
		\item[(A)] For $T'''\xrightarrow{k}T''\xrightarrow{h}T'\xrightarrow{g}T$ the two isomorphisms $k^*h^*g^*\Rightarrow(g\circ h\circ k)^*$ agree:
		\[
		\alpha_{k,\,g h}\circ k^*\alpha_{h,g}=\alpha_{hk,\,g}\circ\alpha_{k,h}\,g^* .
		\]
	\end{enumerate}
	If all $\epsilon_T$ and $\alpha_{h,g}$ are identities, $F$ is a functor $\CatC^{\op}\to\Cat$. If all $F(T)$ are groupoids (resp.\ discrete categories), we speak of a pseudofunctor with values in $\Grpd$ (resp.\ $\Set$).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Convention on coherence isomorphisms]\label{rem:pseudofunctor-convention}
	Conditions~(U) and~(A) imply that any two ways of identifying an iterated pullback $g_n^*\cdots g_1^*\xi$ with $(g_1\circ\dots\circ g_n)^*\xi$ by composites of the $\alpha$'s and $\epsilon$'s agree. Hence, for any two composable strings of morphisms with the same composite, there is a \emph{unique} canonical isomorphism between the corresponding iterated pullbacks. (One way to see this is via the fibred category associated with $F$ (\cite[][Section~3.1.3]{vistoli2007notes}): there all these iterated pullbacks come with cartesian morphisms over the same composite, and are therefore uniquely isomorphic compatibly with them.) In what follows we use these canonical isomorphisms tacitly, and write equalities such as $\mathrm{pr}_{01}^*\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi=q_0^*\xi$. This is a notational convention, not the assumption that $F$ is a strict functor; the one place where the identifications are needed to make sense of a definition is made explicit in Remark~\ref{rem:cocycle-explicit}.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}\label{ex:pseudofunctors}
	\leavevmode
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textbf{Quasi-coherent sheaves.} $T\mapsto\QCoh(T)$, with $g^*$ the pullback of quasi-coherent sheaves and $\alpha_{h,g}:h^*g^*\mathcal{E}\cong(gh)^*\mathcal{E}$ the canonical isomorphisms, is a pseudofunctor $\QCoh:\Sch^{\op}\to\Cat$ (\cite[][Section~3.2.1]{vistoli2007notes}). It is not a functor, since $h^*g^*\mathcal{E}$ and $(gh)^*\mathcal{E}$ are defined by different universal properties.
		\item \textbf{Modules.} $\Specof{A}\mapsto\Mod_A$, with $g^*N=B\otimes_AN$ for $g:\Specof{B}\to\Specof{A}$ and $\alpha_{h,g}:C\otimes_B(B\otimes_AN)\cong C\otimes_AN$ the canonical isomorphisms, is a pseudofunctor $\mathcal{M}:\Aff^{\op}\to\Cat$. The functors $M\mapsto\widetilde{M}$ are equivalences $\Mod_A\simeq\QCoh(\Specof{A})$ compatible with pullback (\cite[][Tags 01IB, 01I9]{stacks-project}), i.e.\ they form a pseudonatural equivalence $\mathcal{M}\simeq\QCoh|_{\Aff}$ in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:desc-pseudonatural}.
		\item \textbf{Presheaves of sets.} A presheaf $F:\CatC^{\op}\to\Set$ is a functor, hence a pseudofunctor with discrete values and trivial coherence isomorphisms.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Grothendieck construction]\label{prop:grothendieck-construction}
	Let $\mathcal C$ be a category. There is an equivalence between
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item categories fibred in groupoids over $\mathcal C$, and
		\item pseudofunctors
		\[
		F : \mathcal C^{op} \to \Grpd.
		\]
	\end{enumerate}
	
	Under this equivalence, a fibred category $\mathcal F \to \mathcal C$
	is sent to the pseudofunctor
	\[
	B \longmapsto \mathcal F(B),
	\]
	which assigns to each object its fibre and to each morphism
	$f : B' \to B$ the pullback functor
	\[
	f^* : \mathcal F(B) \to \mathcal F(B').
	\]
	
	Conversely, a pseudofunctor determines a category fibred in groupoids
	via the Grothendieck construction, see \cite[][Section~3.1.2]{vistoli2007notes} and
	\cite[][Tags 02XJ, 003S]{stacks-project}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
	This construction originates in SGA~1, Exp.~VI, due to
	Alexander Grothendieck, where categories fibred in groupoids are introduced as a framework for descent.
	
	The appearance of pseudofunctors rather than ordinary functors reflects
	the fact that pullbacks in a fibred category are only defined up to
	canonical isomorphism. In particular, for composable morphisms
	\[
	B'' \xrightarrow{f} B' \xrightarrow{g} B
	\]
	one has a natural isomorphism
	\[
	f^* g^* \Rightarrow (g \circ f)^*,
	\]
	and similarly for identities. These isomorphisms satisfy coherence
	conditions, giving rise to a pseudofunctor rather than a strict functor.
	
	From a modern higher-categorical point of view, this is not a defect
	but the correct notion of functoriality. The failure of strict
	functoriality is resolved by passing to homotopy-coherent functors.
	Accordingly, the Grothendieck construction may be viewed as a special
	case of a more general principle, namely that Grothendieck fibrations
	classify functors valued in categories. In higher category theory, this
	extends to the straightening-unstraightening equivalence between
	functors into $\infty$-categories and cartesian fibrations, see
	\cite[Chapter~2.4 and Section~3.2]{lurie2008higher}.
\end{remark}

\section{The descent category}

Let $\CatC$ be a category with fibre products and $f:U\to X$ a morphism. We write
\begin{align*}
	\mathrm{pr}_0,\mathrm{pr}_1 &:U\times_XU \to U \\
	\mathrm{pr}_{01},\mathrm{pr}_{12},\mathrm{pr}_{02} &:U\times_XU\times_XU \to U\times_XU \\
	\mathrm{pr}_0,\mathrm{pr}_1,\mathrm{pr}_2&:U\times_XU\times_XU \to U
\end{align*}

for the projections. In the notation of Definition~\ref{def:cech-nerve}, $\mathrm{pr}_0=d_1$ and $\mathrm{pr}_1=d_0$ on $(U/X)_1$, and $\mathrm{pr}_{01}=d_2$, $\mathrm{pr}_{12}=d_0$, $\mathrm{pr}_{02}=d_1$ on $(U/X)_2$. Moreover $\mathrm{pr}_0\circ\mathrm{pr}_{ij}=q_i$ and $\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\mathrm{pr}_{ij}=q_j$.

\begin{definition}[Descent data]\label{def:classical-descent-data}
	(\cite[][Definition~4.2]{vistoli2007notes}, for a single morphism)
	Let $(\CatC, \mathcal{J})$ be a cite, let $F:\CatC^{\op}\to\Cat$ be a pseudofunctor and $f:U\to X$ a covering in $\CatC$. The \emph{category of descent data along $f$}, denoted $\Desc_f(F)$, is defined as follows.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item An object is a pair $(\xi,\varphi)$ of an object $\xi\in F(U)$ and an isomorphism
		\[
		\varphi:\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi\qquad\text{in }F(U\times_XU)
		\]
		satisfying the \emph{cocycle condition}
		\[
		\mathrm{pr}_{02}^*\varphi=\mathrm{pr}_{12}^*\varphi\circ\mathrm{pr}_{01}^*\varphi\qquad\text{in }F(U\times_XU\times_XU).
		\]
		\item A morphism $(\xi,\varphi)\to(\xi',\varphi')$ is a morphism $a:\xi\to\xi'$ in $F(U)$ such that
		\[
		\varphi'\circ\mathrm{pr}_0^*a=\mathrm{pr}_1^*a\circ\varphi\qquad\text{in }F(U\times_XU).
		\]
	\end{enumerate}
	Composition is composition in $F(U)$. For $F=\QCoh$ we write $\Desc(U/X):=\Desc_f(\QCoh)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical descent data]\label{con:pullback-to-descent}
	In the situation of Definition~\ref{def:classical-descent-data}, let $\zeta\in F(X)$. Since $f\circ\mathrm{pr}_0=f\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$, the coherence isomorphisms give an isomorphism
	\[
	\mathrm{can}_\zeta:=\alpha_{\mathrm{pr}_1,f}(\zeta)^{-1}\circ\alpha_{\mathrm{pr}_0,f}(\zeta):\ \mathrm{pr}_0^*f^*\zeta\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(f\mathrm{pr}_0)^*\zeta=(f\mathrm{pr}_1)^*\zeta\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathrm{pr}_1^*f^*\zeta .
	\]
	The pair $(f^*\zeta,\mathrm{can}_\zeta)$ is an object of $\Desc_f(F)$, and $\zeta\mapsto(f^*\zeta,\mathrm{can}_\zeta)$, $b\mapsto f^*b$, defines a functor, which by abuse of notation we denote
	\[
	f^*:F(X)\longrightarrow\Desc_f(F).
	\]
	We say that $F$ \emph{satisfies descent along $f$} if this functor is an equivalence of categories. Descent data in its essential image are called \emph{effective}.
\end{definition}

Concretely, $f^*$ is fully faithful if and only if morphisms between pullbacks that are compatible with the canonical descent data descend uniquely, and $F$ satisfies descent along $f$ if in addition every descent datum is effective.

\begin{remark}[The normalization is automatic]\label{rem:normalization-automatic}
	Some authors additionally require $\Delta^*\varphi=\mathrm{id}_\xi$, where $\Delta:U\to U\times_XU$ is the diagonal and $\Delta^*\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi=\xi=\Delta^*\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi$. This follows from the cocycle condition: let $\Delta_3:U\to U\times_XU\times_XU$ be the small diagonal. Then $\mathrm{pr}_{ij}\circ\Delta_3=\Delta$ for all $i<j$, so pulling the cocycle condition back along $\Delta_3$ gives $\Delta^*\varphi=\Delta^*\varphi\circ\Delta^*\varphi$. Since $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, $\Delta^*\varphi=\mathrm{id}_\xi$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sets, groupoids and categories]\label{rem:desc-sets}
	Definition~\ref{def:classical-descent-data} contains the sheaf condition of Chapter~\ref{chap:grothendieck-topologies} and the descent condition for stacks as special cases.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textbf{Sets.} Let $F:\CatC^{\op}\to\Set$ be a presheaf, regarded as a pseudofunctor with discrete values. An isomorphism $\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi\to\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi$ in the discrete category $F(U\times_XU)$ exists if and only if $\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi=\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi$, and then it is the identity; the cocycle condition is automatic. Hence $\Desc_f(F)$ is the discrete category on $\Eq\bigl(F(U)\rightrightarrows F(U\times_XU)\bigr)$, and $F$ satisfies descent along $f$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{con:pullback-to-descent} if and only if it does so in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:cech-descent}, by Proposition~\ref{prop:cech-descent-equalizer}.
		\item \textbf{Groupoids.} If $F$ takes values in $\Grpd$, the gluing isomorphisms are additional data and the cocycle condition is a genuine condition. Every morphism of descent data is invertible, so $\Desc_f(F)$ is a groupoid. This is the situation of Definition~\ref{def:stack}.
		\item \textbf{Categories.} If $F$ takes values in $\Cat$, as for $\QCoh$, objects of $\Desc_f(F)$ are described exactly as for groupoids, but morphisms of descent data need not be invertible.
	\end{enumerate}
	Thus the passage from sets to groupoids replaces equalities on $U\times_XU$ by isomorphisms and the cocycle condition on $U\times_XU\times_XU$.
\end{remark}

The following functoriality is used to compare $\QCoh$ with modules and with affine morphisms.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:desc-pseudonatural}
	Let $F,G:\CatC^{\op}\to\Cat$ be pseudofunctors and $f:U\to X$ a morphism in $\CatC$.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Let $\Phi:F\Rightarrow G$ be a \emph{pseudonatural transformation}, i.e.\ functors $\Phi_T:F(T)\to G(T)$ together with natural isomorphisms $\beta_g:g^*\Phi_T\Rightarrow\Phi_{T'}g^*$ for $g:T'\to T$, compatible with the coherence isomorphisms: $\beta_{gh}\circ\alpha^G_{h,g}\Phi_T=\Phi_{T''}\alpha^F_{h,g}\circ\beta_hg^*\circ h^*\beta_g$ for $T''\xrightarrow{h}T'\xrightarrow{g}T$, and similarly for $\epsilon$. 
		
		Then
		\begin{align*}
			\Phi_*:\Desc_f(F) &\longrightarrow\Desc_f(G) \\
			(\xi,\varphi) &\longmapsto\bigl(\Phi_U\xi, \beta_{\mathrm{pr}_1}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{U\times_XU}(\varphi)\circ\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_0}\bigr)\\
			a &\longmapsto\Phi_U(a),
		\end{align*}
		is a functor, and $\beta_f$ induces an isomorphism $\Phi_*\circ f^*\cong f^*\circ\Phi_X$. If every $\Phi_T$ is an equivalence of categories, then so is $\Phi_*$. In this case $F$ satisfies descent along $f$ if and only if $G$ does.
		\item Let $F^{\op}$ denote the pseudofunctor $T\mapsto F(T)^{\op}$ with the same pullback functors and the inverse coherence isomorphisms. Then $(\xi,\varphi)\mapsto(\xi,\varphi^{-1})$ is an isomorphism of categories $\Desc_f(F^{\op})\cong\Desc_f(F)^{\op}$, compatible with $f^*$.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	(1) The cocycle condition for $\Phi_*(\xi,\varphi)$ follows by applying $\Phi_{U\times_XU\times_XU}$ to the cocycle condition for $\varphi$ and using the compatibility of $\beta$ with the $\alpha$'s to move the $\beta$'s past the identifications of Remark~\ref{rem:cocycle-explicit}. The compatibility of $\Phi_U(a)$ with the descent data follows from naturality of $\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_0}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_1}$, and the isomorphism $\Phi_*f^*\zeta\cong f^*\Phi_X\zeta$ is $\beta_f(\zeta):f^*\Phi_X\zeta\to\Phi_Uf^*\zeta$, which is compatible with the canonical descent data by the compatibility of $\beta$ with the $\alpha$'s.
	
	Now assume that every $\Phi_T$ is an equivalence. \emph{Faithful:} $\Phi_U$ is faithful. \emph{Full:} a morphism $\Phi_U\xi\to\Phi_U\xi'$ of descent data is $\Phi_U(a)$ for a unique $a:\xi\to\xi'$, and $a$ is compatible with $\varphi,\varphi'$ because $\Phi_{U\times_XU}$ is faithful and the compatibility holds after applying it (conjugated by $\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_0},\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_1}$). \emph{Essentially surjective:} let $(\eta,\psi)\in\Desc_f(G)$. Choose $\xi\in F(U)$ and an isomorphism $u:\Phi_U\xi\to\eta$. Transporting $\psi$ along $u$ and conjugating with $\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_0},\beta_{\mathrm{pr}_1}$ gives an isomorphism $\Phi_{U\times_XU}(\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi)\to\Phi_{U\times_XU}(\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi)$, which is $\Phi_{U\times_XU}(\varphi)$ for a unique isomorphism $\varphi$ since $\Phi_{U\times_XU}$ is fully faithful. The cocycle condition for $\varphi$ holds since it holds after applying the faithful functor $\Phi_{U\times_XU\times_XU}$, and by construction $u$ is an isomorphism $\Phi_*(\xi,\varphi)\cong(\eta,\psi)$. The last claim follows from $\Phi_*f^*\cong f^*\Phi_X$ and the two-out-of-three property of equivalences.
	
	(2) An object of $\Desc_f(F^{\op})$ consists of $\xi$ and an isomorphism $\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi\to\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi$ in $F(U\times_XU)^{\op}$, i.e.\ an isomorphism $\varphi:\mathrm{pr}_1^*\xi\to\mathrm{pr}_0^*\xi$ in $F(U\times_XU)$, and the cocycle condition in $F^{\op}$ reads $\varphi_{02}=\varphi_{01}\circ\varphi_{12}$, which is equivalent to $\varphi_{02}^{-1}=\varphi_{12}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{01}^{-1}$. Morphisms are reversed in the same way.
\end{proof}

\section{The Amitsur complex}

Let $R\to S$ be a ring map, $X=\Specof{R}$ and $U=\Specof{S}$. Since fibre products of affine schemes are computed by tensor products, $\check{C}(U/X)_n=\Specof{S^{\otimes_R(n+1)}}$. Applying $\Gamma(-,\OO)$ to the \v{C}ech nerve therefore gives a cosimplicial ring.

\begin{definition}[Amitsur complex]\label{def:amitsur}
	(\cite[][Section~023F]{stacks-project})
	Let $(S/R)^\bullet$ be the cosimplicial $R$-algebra with $(S/R)^n=S^{\otimes_R(n+1)}$, where $\theta:[m]\to[n]$ acts by
	\[
	s_0\otimes\dots\otimes s_m\longmapsto t_0\otimes\dots\otimes t_n,\qquad t_j=\prod_{\theta(i)=j}s_i .
	\]
	In particular, the coface $d^i:(S/R)^{n-1}\to(S/R)^n$ inserts a $1$ in position $i$, dual to the face $d_i$ of the \v{C}ech nerve forgetting the $i$-th factor. For an $R$-module $M$, the cosimplicial $(S/R)^\bullet$-module $(S/R)^\bullet\otimes_RM$ is augmented by $\varepsilon:M\to S\otimes_RM$, $m\mapsto1\otimes m$. The associated cochain complex
	\[
	0\to M\xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ }S\otimes_RM\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }S\otimes_RS\otimes_RM\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }\cdots,\qquad\partial=\sum_{i}(-1)^id^i,
	\]
	is the \emph{(augmented) Amitsur complex} of $M$.
\end{definition}

The homotopical input is the following standard fact: an augmented cosimplicial object with an extra codegeneracy is contractible. To state it uniformly, regard the augmentation as the coface $d^0:M^{-1}\to M^0$.

\begin{lemma}[Extra codegeneracy]\label{lem:extra-codegeneracy}
	Let $M^{-1}\xrightarrow{d^0}M^\bullet$ be an augmented cosimplicial abelian group, with cofaces $d^0,\dots,d^n:M^{n-1}\to M^n$ for $n\ge0$. Suppose there are homomorphisms $h:M^n\to M^{n-1}$ for $n\ge0$ such that
	\[
	h\circ d^0=\mathrm{id}_{M^{n-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad h\circ d^i=d^{i-1}\circ h\quad(1\le i\le n)
	\]
	as maps $M^{n-1}\to M^{n-1}$, for all $n\ge0$. Then $h\partial+\partial h=\mathrm{id}$ on $M^n$ for $n\ge0$ and $h\partial=\mathrm{id}$ on $M^{-1}$. In particular the augmented complex $0\to M^{-1}\to M^0\to M^1\to\cdots$ is exact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	On $M^{-1}$ we have $h\partial=hd^0=\mathrm{id}$. For $x\in M^n$ with $n\ge0$,
	\[
	h\partial x=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^ihd^ix=x+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^id^{i-1}hx=x-\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^jd^jhx=x-\partial hx .
	\]
	So $h$ is a contracting homotopy, and a contractible complex is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The split case]\label{lem:amitsur-split}
	(\cite[][Lemma~35.3.5, Tag 023F]{stacks-project})
	Suppose $R\to S$ has a retraction $\sigma:S\to R$ of $R$-algebras. Then for every $R$-module $M$ the augmented Amitsur complex is exact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	Define $h:S^{\otimes_R(n+1)}\otimes_RM\to S^{\otimes_Rn}\otimes_RM$ by
	\[
	h(s_0\otimes s_1\otimes\dots\otimes s_n\otimes m)=\sigma(s_0)s_1\otimes\dots\otimes s_n\otimes m,
	\]
	and $h(s_0\otimes m)=\sigma(s_0)m$ for $n=0$. This is well defined because the expression is $R$-multilinear. Since $d^0$ inserts $1$ in position $0$ and $\sigma(1)=1$, we get $h\circ d^0=\mathrm{id}$. For $i\ge1$, $d^i$ inserts $1$ in position $i\ge1$, which commutes with multiplying $\sigma(s_0)\in R$ into the next factor, so $h\circ d^i=d^{i-1}\circ h$. Lemma~\ref{lem:extra-codegeneracy} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Amitsur]\label{thm:amitsur}
	(\cite[][Lemma~35.3.6, Tag 023M]{stacks-project})
	Let $R\to S$ be faithfully flat. Then for every $R$-module $M$ the augmented Amitsur complex is exact. In particular
	\[
	M\xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ }S\otimes_RM\ \substack{\xrightarrow{\ d^0\ }\\[-0.2em]\xrightarrow[\ d^1\ ]{}}\ S\otimes_RS\otimes_RM,\qquad d^0(s\otimes m)=1\otimes s\otimes m,\quad d^1(s\otimes m)=s\otimes1\otimes m,
	\]
	is an equalizer diagram.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	Let $C(M)$ denote the augmented Amitsur complex of $M$ over $R$. Base change along $R\to S$: for the $S$-algebra $S':=S\otimes_RS$ (via $s\mapsto s\otimes1$) and the $S$-module $S\otimes_RM$ there are isomorphisms
	\[
	S\otimes_R\bigl(S^{\otimes_R(n+1)}\otimes_RM\bigr)\cong S'^{\otimes_S(n+1)}\otimes_S(S\otimes_RM),
	\]
	natural in $[n]$ and compatible with the augmentations. So $S\otimes_RC(M)$ is the augmented Amitsur complex of $S\otimes_RM$ for $S\to S'$. The multiplication $\mu:S\otimes_RS\to S$ is a retraction of $S\to S'$ as $S$-algebras, so $S\otimes_RC(M)$ is exact by Lemma~\ref{lem:amitsur-split}. Since $S$ is flat over $R$, $H^i(S\otimes_RC(M))=S\otimes_RH^i(C(M))$, and since $S$ is faithfully flat, $S\otimes_RH^i(C(M))=0$ implies $H^i(C(M))=0$ (\cite[][Tag 00HP]{stacks-project}). The last claim is exactness in degrees $-1$ and $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Homotopical meaning]\label{rem:amitsur-homotopical}
	Geometrically, $\Specof{\mu}$ is the diagonal $U\to U\times_XU$, a section of the base change $\mathrm{pr}_0:U\times_XU\to U$ of $f$ along itself. The augmented \v{C}ech nerve of a morphism with a section has an extra degeneracy and is therefore simplicially contractible (\cite[][Tag 019Z]{stacks-project}). Theorem~\ref{thm:amitsur} says: \emph{after the faithfully flat base change along $f$ itself, the \v{C}ech nerve of $f$ becomes contractible, and faithfully flat base change detects exactness.} Only exactness in degrees $\le0$ is needed for descent. The higher exactness says that quasi-coherent sheaves have vanishing \v{C}ech cohomology for affine fpqc coverings, which we will not need.
\end{remark}

\section{Faithfully flat descent for modules}
%==========================================================

Let $R\to S$ be a ring map and $f:U=\Specof{S}\to X=\Specof{R}$. We apply Definition~\ref{def:classical-descent-data} to the pseudofunctor $\mathcal{M}$ of Example~\ref{ex:pseudofunctors}(2) and write
\[
\Desc(S/R):=\Desc_f(\mathcal{M}).
\]
Here $U\times_XU=\Specof{S\otimes_RS}$, and $\mathrm{pr}_0=d_1$, $\mathrm{pr}_1=d_0$ correspond to the ring maps $d^1(s)=s\otimes1$ and $d^0(s)=1\otimes s$. Hence $\mathrm{pr}_0^*N=N\otimes_RS$ and $\mathrm{pr}_1^*N=S\otimes_RN$ for an $S$-module $N$, and an object of $\Desc(S/R)$ is a pair $(N,\varphi)$ of an $S$-module $N$ and an $S\otimes_RS$-linear isomorphism
\[
\varphi:N\otimes_RS\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }S\otimes_RN\qquad\text{with}\qquad\varphi_{02}=\varphi_{12}\circ\varphi_{01}.
\]
Here $\varphi_{01}=\varphi\otimes\mathrm{id}_S$, $\varphi_{12}=\mathrm{id}_S\otimes\varphi$, and $\varphi_{02}$ is the base change of $\varphi$ along $d^1:S\otimes_RS\to S^{\otimes_R3}$, $s\otimes t\mapsto s\otimes1\otimes t$, since $\mathrm{pr}_{01},\mathrm{pr}_{12},\mathrm{pr}_{02}$ correspond to $d^2,d^0,d^1$. Morphisms are $S$-linear maps $a:N\to N'$ with $\varphi'\circ(a\otimes\mathrm{id}_S)=(\mathrm{id}_S\otimes a)\circ\varphi$. This is exactly \cite[][Definition~35.3.1, Tag 023F]{stacks-project}. For an $R$-module $M$, the canonical descent datum of Definition~\ref{con:pullback-to-descent} is
\begin{align*}
	f^*M=(S\otimes_RM,\mathrm{can})\\
	\mathrm{can}:(S\otimes_RM)\otimes_RS\longrightarrow S\otimes_R(S\otimes_RM),\quad s\otimes m\otimes t\longmapsto s\otimes t\otimes m .
\end{align*}

For a descent datum $(N,\varphi)$ we put
\[
N^\varphi:=\{\,n\in N\mid\varphi(n\otimes1)=1\otimes n\,\}=\Eq\Bigl(N\ \substack{\xrightarrow{\ n\mapsto\varphi(n\otimes1)\ }\\[-0.2em]\xrightarrow[\ n\mapsto1\otimes n\ ]{}}\ S\otimes_RN\Bigr),
\]
an $R$-submodule of $N$. If $a:(N,\varphi)\to(N',\varphi')$ is a morphism, then $\varphi'(a(n)\otimes1)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes a)\varphi(n\otimes1)=1\otimes a(n)$ for $n\in N^\varphi$, so $a$ restricts to $N^\varphi\to N'^{\varphi'}$. This defines a functor $(-)^\varphi:\Desc(S/R)\to\Mod_R$.

\begin{remark}
	Under Remark~\ref{rem:desc-2-truncation}, $(N,\varphi)$ corresponds to a cosimplicial module $N^\bullet$ with $N^0=N$, and under $N^1\cong S\otimes_RN$ the two cofaces $N^0\to N^1$ are $n\mapsto1\otimes n$ and $n\mapsto\varphi(n\otimes1)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:tot-cosimplicial-set}, applied to underlying sets, $N^\varphi=\lim_\Delta N^\bullet$. So the quasi-inverse in Theorem~\ref{thm:faithfully-flat-descent-modules} is the totalization, and the theorem says that the adjunction between $R$-modules and descent data, i.e.\ between constant cosimplicial modules and their totalization, is an equivalence. We do not use this.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Faithfully flat descent for modules]\label{thm:faithfully-flat-descent-modules}
	(\cite[][Proposition~35.3.9, Tag 023N]{stacks-project})
	Let $R\to S$ be faithfully flat and $f:\Specof{S}\to\Specof{R}$. Then
	\[
	f^*:\Mod_R\longrightarrow\Desc(S/R),\qquad M\longmapsto(S\otimes_RM,\mathrm{can}),
	\]
	is an equivalence of categories with quasi-inverse $(N,\varphi)\mapsto N^\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	It suffices to construct natural isomorphisms $\eta:\mathrm{id}\Rightarrow(-)^\varphi\circ f^*$ and $\epsilon:f^*\circ(-)^\varphi\Rightarrow\mathrm{id}$.
	
	\emph{Unit.} Let $M$ be an $R$-module. For $x\in S\otimes_RM$ we have $\mathrm{can}(x\otimes1)=d^1(x)$ and $1\otimes x=d^0(x)$ in $S\otimes_RS\otimes_RM$, with $d^0,d^1$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:amitsur}. Hence $(S\otimes_RM)^{\mathrm{can}}=\Eq(d^0,d^1)$, and $\eta_M:M\to(S\otimes_RM)^{\mathrm{can}}$, $m\mapsto1\otimes m$, is an isomorphism by Theorem~\ref{thm:amitsur}.
	
	\emph{Counit.} Let $(N,\varphi)$ be a descent datum and $M:=N^\varphi$. Define
	\[
	\epsilon:S\otimes_RM\longrightarrow N,\qquad s\otimes m\longmapsto sm .
	\]
	It is a morphism of descent data $(S\otimes_RM,\mathrm{can})\to(N,\varphi)$: for $s,t\in S$ and $m\in M$, $S\otimes_RS$-linearity of $\varphi$ gives
	\begin{align*}
			\varphi\bigl((\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_S)(s\otimes m\otimes t)\bigr) &= \varphi\bigl((s\otimes t)\cdot(m\otimes1)\bigr)=(s\otimes t)\cdot(1\otimes m) \\
			&= s\otimes tm = (\mathrm{id}_S\otimes\epsilon)\bigl(\mathrm{can}(s\otimes m\otimes t)\bigr).
	\end{align*}

	It remains to show that $\epsilon$ is bijective.
	
	Since $S$ is flat over $R$, applying $-\otimes_RS$ to the equalizer defining $M$ gives the equalizer in the top row of
	\[
	\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
		M\otimes_RS \arrow[r] & N\otimes_RS \arrow[r, shift left, "a_0"] \arrow[r, shift right, "a_1"'] \arrow[d, "\varphi"', "\cong"] & S\otimes_RN\otimes_RS \arrow[d, "\varphi_{12}", "\cong"'] \\
		N \arrow[r, "\varepsilon"] & S\otimes_RN \arrow[r, shift left, "d^0"] \arrow[r, shift right, "d^1"'] & S\otimes_RS\otimes_RN
	\end{tikzcd}
	\]
	with $a_0(n\otimes s)=1\otimes n\otimes s$ and $a_1(n\otimes s)=\varphi(n\otimes1)\otimes s$. The bottom row is the beginning of the augmented Amitsur complex of the $R$-module $N$, hence an equalizer by Theorem~\ref{thm:amitsur}. Both squares commute:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\varphi_{12}\,a_0(n\otimes s)&=1\otimes\varphi(n\otimes s)=d^0\varphi(n\otimes s),\\
		\varphi_{12}\,a_1(n\otimes s)&=\varphi_{12}\varphi_{01}(n\otimes1\otimes s)=\varphi_{02}(n\otimes1\otimes s)=d^1\varphi(n\otimes s),
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where the second computation uses the cocycle condition and the definition of $\varphi_{02}$ as a base change along $d^1$. So the vertical isomorphisms identify the two parallel pairs, and hence their equalizers: $\varphi$ restricts to an isomorphism from $M\otimes_RS$ onto $\varepsilon(N)\cong N$. For $m\in M$ and $s\in S$, $S\otimes_RS$-linearity of $\varphi$ gives
	\[
	\varphi(m\otimes s)=(1\otimes s)\cdot\varphi(m\otimes1)=(1\otimes s)\cdot(1\otimes m)=1\otimes sm=\varepsilon(sm).
	\]
	Hence the induced isomorphism $M\otimes_RS\to N$ is $m\otimes s\mapsto sm$, i.e.\ $\epsilon$ is bijective (after the symmetry $M\otimes_RS\cong S\otimes_RM$).
	
	Naturality of $\eta$ and $\epsilon$ is clear from the formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
	The proof uses exactly two inputs: the Amitsur complex is exact in degrees $\le0$ (for the unit, and for the $R$-module $N$ in the counit), and $-\otimes_RS$ preserves equalizers (flatness). Faithful flatness is used only through Theorem~\ref{thm:amitsur}.
\end{remark}

\section{fpqc descent for quasi-coherent sheaves}
%==========================================================

We now globalize. For a morphism $f:U\to X$ of schemes, an object of $\Desc(U/X)=\Desc_f(\QCoh)$ is, by Definition~\ref{def:classical-descent-data}, a pair $(\mathcal{E},\varphi)$ of $\mathcal{E}\in\QCoh(U)$ and an isomorphism $\varphi:\mathrm{pr}_0^*\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{pr}_1^*\mathcal{E}$ on $U\times_XU$ with $\mathrm{pr}_{02}^*\varphi=\mathrm{pr}_{12}^*\varphi\circ\mathrm{pr}_{01}^*\varphi$, and morphisms are maps $a:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}'$ with $\varphi'\circ\mathrm{pr}_0^*a=\mathrm{pr}_1^*a\circ\varphi$. In particular, for $\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\QCoh(X)$, $f^*$ is bijective on $\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})$ if and only if
\[
\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\ f^*\ }\Hom(f^*\mathcal{F},f^*\mathcal{G})\ \substack{\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{pr}_0^*\ }\\[-0.2em]\xrightarrow[\ \mathrm{pr}_1^*\ ]{}}\ \Hom\bigl((f\mathrm{pr}_0)^*\mathcal{F},(f\mathrm{pr}_0)^*\mathcal{G}\bigr)
\]
is an equalizer. To keep the formulas readable we use the following convention: on a fibre product of copies of $U$ and $U'$, an index such as $\varphi_{u_0u}$ denotes the pullback of $\varphi$ along the projection to the indicated two factors (composed with $g:U'\to U$ where necessary).

\begin{lemma}[Flat pullback is faithful]\label{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}
	Let $g:T'\to T$ be flat and surjective. Then $g^*:\Mod(\OO_T)\to\Mod(\OO_{T'})$ is faithful.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	Since $g^*$ is additive, it suffices to show that $g^*u=0$ implies $u=0$ for $u:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}$. Let $x\in T$ and choose $x'\in T'$ with $g(x')=x$. Then $(g^*u)_{x'}=u_x\otimes_{\OO_{T,x}}\OO_{T',x'}$, and $\OO_{T,x}\to\OO_{T',x'}$ is a flat local homomorphism, hence faithfully flat (\cite[][Tag 00HR]{stacks-project}). By flatness $\mathrm{im}(u_x)\otimes\OO_{T',x'}=\mathrm{im}(u_x\otimes\OO_{T',x'})=0$, hence $\mathrm{im}(u_x)=0$ by faithful flatness (\cite[][Tag 00HP]{stacks-project}). So $u_x=0$ for all $x$, i.e.\ $u=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The affine case]\label{lem:qcoh-descent-affine}
	Let $h:\Specof{S}\to\Specof{R}$ be flat and surjective. Then $\QCoh$ satisfies descent along $h$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	The ring map $R\to S$ is faithfully flat (\cite[][Tag 00HQ]{stacks-project}), and $\Specof{S}\times_{\Specof{R}}\Specof{S}=\Specof{S\otimes_RS}$. The pseudonatural equivalence $\mathcal{M}\simeq\QCoh|_{\Aff}$, $M\mapsto\widetilde{M}$, of Example~\ref{ex:pseudofunctors}(2) induces, by Lemma~\ref{lem:desc-pseudonatural}(1), an equivalence $\Desc(S/R)\simeq\Desc_h(\QCoh)$ compatible with the canonical functors from $\Mod_R\simeq\QCoh(\Specof{R})$. Now apply Theorem~\ref{thm:faithfully-flat-descent-modules}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[fpqc descent for quasi-coherent sheaves]\label{thm:fpqc-descent-qcoh}
	(\cite[][Proposition~35.5.2, Tag 023T]{stacks-project})
	Let $f:U\to X$ be an fpqc covering of schemes. Then
	\[
	f^*:\QCoh(X)\longrightarrow\Desc(U/X),\qquad\mathcal{F}\longmapsto(f^*\mathcal{F},\mathrm{can}),
	\]
	is an equivalence of categories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	Throughout, we use that base changes of fpqc coverings are fpqc coverings (Proposition~\ref{prop:gross-topology-chain}), in particular they are flat and surjective.
	
	\emph{Step 1: $f^*$ is fully faithful if $X$ is affine.} Faithfulness is Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}. For fullness, let $\beta:f^*\mathcal{F}\to f^*\mathcal{G}$ with $\mathrm{pr}_0^*\beta=\mathrm{pr}_1^*\beta$. By Definition~\ref{def:fpqc-covering} there are affine opens $W_1,\dots,W_r\subset U$ with $X=\bigcup_kf(W_k)$. Then $U':=\coprod_kW_k$ is affine, and with $g:U'\to U$ the induced morphism, $h:=f\circ g:U'\to X$ is a flat surjective morphism of affine schemes. The pullback $g^*\beta$ is a morphism in $\Desc(U'/X)$ (pull back the compatibility along $g\times g$), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:qcoh-descent-affine} there is $\alpha:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}$ with $h^*\alpha=g^*\beta$. To see $f^*\alpha=\beta$, let $q:P:=U'\times_XU\to U$ be the second projection, which is flat and surjective as a base change of $h$. With $p_1:P\to U'$ the first projection we get
	\[
	q^*f^*\alpha=p_1^*h^*\alpha=p_1^*g^*\beta=(g\times\mathrm{id})^*\mathrm{pr}_0^*\beta=(g\times\mathrm{id})^*\mathrm{pr}_1^*\beta=q^*\beta,
	\]
	hence $f^*\alpha=\beta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}.
	
	\emph{Step 2: $f^*$ is fully faithful in general.} Let $X=\bigcup_jX_j$ be an affine open cover and $U_j:=f^{-1}(X_j)$. Given $\beta$ as in Step 1, Step 1 for the fpqc covering $U_j\to X_j$ gives $\alpha_j:\mathcal{F}|_{X_j}\to\mathcal{G}|_{X_j}$ with $f^*\alpha_j=\beta|_{U_j}$. The restrictions of $\alpha_j$ and $\alpha_k$ to $X_j\cap X_k$ have the same pullback along the flat surjection $f^{-1}(X_j\cap X_k)\to X_j\cap X_k$, so they agree by Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}. Hence they glue to $\alpha:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}$ (\cite[][Lemma~6.33.1, Tag 00AK]{stacks-project}), and $f^*\alpha=\beta$ because this holds on every $U_j$. Faithfulness is again Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}.
	
	\emph{Step 3: effectivity if $X$ is affine.} Let $(\mathcal{E},\varphi)\in\Desc(U/X)$ and choose $g:U'\to U$, $h=fg$ as in Step 1. Then $(g^*\mathcal{E},(g\times g)^*\varphi)\in\Desc(U'/X)$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:qcoh-descent-affine} there are $\mathcal{F}\in\QCoh(X)$ and an isomorphism $a:h^*\mathcal{F}\to g^*\mathcal{E}$ of descent data. With the index convention above, the latter says $a_{u'_1}=\varphi_{gu'_0\,gu'_1}\circ a_{u'_0}$ on $U'\times_XU'$. On $P=U'\times_XU$ consider the isomorphism
	\[
	b:=\varphi_{gu'\,u}\circ a_{u'}:\ q^*f^*\mathcal{F}=p_1^*h^*\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow q^*\mathcal{E}.
	\]
	The two pullbacks of $b$ to $P\times_UP=U'\times_XU'\times_XU$ (points $(u'_0,u'_1,u)$) agree:
	\[
	\varphi_{gu'_1\,u}\circ a_{u'_1}=\varphi_{gu'_1\,u}\circ\varphi_{gu'_0\,gu'_1}\circ a_{u'_0}=\varphi_{gu'_0\,u}\circ a_{u'_0},
	\]
	by the compatibility of $a$ and the cocycle condition. Hence $b$ is a morphism in $\Desc(P/U)$ between the canonical descent data of $f^*\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{E}$, and since $\{q\}$ is an fpqc covering, Step 2 gives a unique $c:f^*\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}$ with $q^*c=b$. Applying the same to $b^{-1}$ shows that $c$ is an isomorphism. Finally, $c$ is a morphism of descent data, i.e.\ $\varphi_{u_0u_1}\circ c_{u_0}=c_{u_1}$ on $U\times_XU$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful} this may be checked after pulling back to $U'\times_XU\times_XU$ (points $(u',u_0,u_1)$), where it becomes $\varphi_{u_0u_1}\circ\varphi_{gu'\,u_0}\circ a_{u'}=\varphi_{gu'\,u_1}\circ a_{u'}$, which is again the cocycle condition.
	
	\emph{Step 4: effectivity in general.} Let $(\mathcal{E},\varphi)\in\Desc(U/X)$ and let $X_j$, $U_j$ be as in Step 2. By Step 3 there are $\mathcal{F}_j\in\QCoh(X_j)$ and isomorphisms $c_j:f^*\mathcal{F}_j\to\mathcal{E}|_{U_j}$ of descent data. On $X_{jk}:=X_j\cap X_k$, the isomorphism $c_k^{-1}\circ c_j$ is an isomorphism of descent data along $f^{-1}(X_{jk})\to X_{jk}$, so by Step 2 there is a unique isomorphism $\theta_{jk}:\mathcal{F}_j|_{X_{jk}}\to\mathcal{F}_k|_{X_{jk}}$ with $f^*\theta_{jk}=c_k^{-1}c_j$. By uniqueness, $\theta_{kl}\circ\theta_{jk}=\theta_{jl}$ on triple intersections. So the $\mathcal{F}_j$ glue to an $\OO_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$ (\cite[][Lemmas~6.33.2 and 6.33.3, Tag 00AK]{stacks-project}), which is quasi-coherent since quasi-coherence is local. The $c_j$ agree on overlaps by construction, hence glue to an isomorphism $c:f^*\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}$, and $c$ is compatible with the descent data because this can be checked on the open cover of $U\times_XU$ by the preimages of the $X_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
	The structure of the proof is that of \cite[][Lemma~4.25]{vistoli2007notes}: Zariski gluing together with the affine case suffices. The only non-formal inputs are Theorem~\ref{thm:faithfully-flat-descent-modules} and Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-pullback-faithful}. In Step~3 we avoid comparing the descent categories for $U$ and $U'$ directly: we descend the comparison isomorphism along the auxiliary covering $U'\times_XU\to U$, which only requires the fully faithful part. Combined with the passage from covering families to single morphisms (as in Proposition~\ref{lem:sheaf-single-cover}, see \cite[][Lemma~4.25, Step~2]{vistoli2007notes}), Theorem~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-qcoh} shows that $\QCoh$ is a stack for the fpqc topology in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:stack} (applied to the fibred category associated with $\QCoh$ by the Grothendieck construction), except that its fibres are categories rather than groupoids.
\end{remark}

\section{Descent of affine morphisms}
%==========================================================

Pullback of quasi-coherent sheaves is strong monoidal: $f^*(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})\cong f^*\mathcal{F}\otimes f^*\mathcal{G}$ and $f^*\OO_X\cong\OO_U$, compatibly with the canonical isomorphisms. Hence $T\mapsto\QCohAlg(T)$, with the pullback functors and coherence isomorphisms of Example~\ref{ex:pseudofunctors}(1), is a pseudofunctor $\QCohAlg:\Sch^{\op}\to\Cat$; the coherence isomorphisms are algebra isomorphisms. An object of $\Desc_f(\QCohAlg)$ is an object $(\mathcal{B}',\varphi)$ of $\Desc_f(\QCoh)$ such that $\mathcal{B}'$ is a quasi-coherent $\OO_U$-algebra and $\varphi$ is an algebra isomorphism, and morphisms are morphisms of descent data that are algebra maps. Similarly, let $\mathcal{A}\mathit{ff}:\Sch^{\op}\to\Cat$ be the pseudofunctor sending $T$ to the category of affine morphisms $V\to T$ (with morphisms over $T$), and $g:T'\to T$ to the base change $V\mapsto V\times_TT'$ for a chosen fibre product; the coherence isomorphisms are the canonical isomorphisms between iterated fibre products given by their universal properties.

\begin{corollary}[fpqc descent of affine morphisms]\label{thm:fpqc-descent-affine-schemes}
	(\cite[][Lemma~35.37.1, Tag 0245]{stacks-project})
	Let $f:U\to X$ be an fpqc covering morphism of schemes. Then
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item $f^*:\QCohAlg(X)\to\Desc_f(\QCohAlg)$ is an equivalence of categories, and
		\item $f^*:\{\text{affine morphisms }V\to X\}\to\Desc_f(\mathcal{A}\mathit{ff})$, $V\mapsto V\times_XU$, is an equivalence of categories.
	\end{enumerate}
	In other words, every affine $U$-scheme $V'$ with an isomorphism $\varphi:V'\times_{U,\mathrm{pr}_0}(U\times_XU)\cong(U\times_XU)\times_{\mathrm{pr}_1,U}V'$ over $U\times_XU$ satisfying the cocycle condition is isomorphic, compatibly with $\varphi$, to $V\times_XU$ for an affine $X$-scheme $V$, which is unique up to unique isomorphism; and morphisms of affine $X$-schemes descend uniquely.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
	(1) Full faithfulness: an algebra map between quasi-coherent algebras on $X$ is a morphism of modules compatible with multiplication and unit. By Theorem~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-qcoh} a morphism of descent data of algebras descends uniquely to a module map, and it is compatible with multiplication and unit because it is so after applying the faithful functor $f^*$. Effectivity: let $(\mathcal{B}',\varphi)$ be a descent datum of algebras. By Theorem~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-qcoh} there is $\mathcal{B}\in\QCoh(X)$ with $f^*\mathcal{B}\cong\mathcal{B}'$ compatibly with the descent data. Since $\varphi$ is an algebra isomorphism, multiplication $\mathcal{B}'\otimes\mathcal{B}'\to\mathcal{B}'$ and unit $\OO_U\to\mathcal{B}'$ are morphisms of descent data, where $\mathcal{B}'\otimes\mathcal{B}'\cong f^*(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B})$ and $\OO_U\cong f^*\OO_X$ carry the canonical data. By full faithfulness they descend to $\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}$ and $\OO_X\to\mathcal{B}$, and associativity, commutativity and unitality hold because they hold after applying the faithful functor $f^*$.
	
	(2) The relative spectrum $V\mapsto(V\to T)_*\OO_V$ is an anti-equivalence between affine morphisms $V\to T$ and quasi-coherent $\OO_T$-algebras, compatible with arbitrary base change (\cite[][Lemma~29.11.5, Section~01S5]{stacks-project} and \cite[][Lemma~27.4.6, Tag 01LX]{stacks-project}). Hence the relative spectrum is a pseudonatural equivalence $\QCohAlg^{\op}\simeq\mathcal{A}\mathit{ff}$, where $\QCohAlg^{\op}$ is the fibrewise opposite. By Lemma~\ref{lem:desc-pseudonatural} we obtain equivalences $\Desc_f(\mathcal{A}\mathit{ff})\simeq\Desc_f(\QCohAlg^{\op})\cong\Desc_f(\QCohAlg)^{\op}$, compatible with the canonical functors from $\{\text{affine morphisms }V\to X\}\simeq\QCohAlg(X)^{\op}$. Now apply (1).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is quoted rather than proved]\label{rem:descent-quoted}
	Later chapters use, in addition, the following descent statements, which we quote:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item descent of open subsets along fpqc coverings (\cite[][Tag 03N0]{stacks-project}, Lemma~\ref{lem:descent-open-subsets});
		\item effective descent for quasi-affine morphisms (\cite[][Tag 0247]{stacks-project}) and, for fppf coverings, for separated and locally quasi-finite morphisms (\cite[][Tag 02W8]{stacks-project}); the proofs in loc.\ cit.\ build on Corollary~\ref{thm:fpqc-descent-affine-schemes};
		\item ``being an isomorphism'' is fpqc local on the base (\cite[][Tag 02L4]{stacks-project}).
	\end{enumerate}
	Item~(2) is what makes the quotient of a scheme by an \etale\ equivalence relation an algebraic space; this, and not descent of affine schemes, is what glues the local coarse moduli spaces in the proof of the Keel--Mori theorem.
\end{remark}
