I would like to dedicate a few words to Tim Lichtenau.
He was a constant source of inspiration and intuition throughout my entire bachelor’s studies. We started studying together, and both quickly became fascinated by category theory. While I was diving into simplicial homotopy theory and model theory, he was already exploring algebraic geometry. He would often speak enthusiastically about deeper connections via infinity stacks. At the time, lacking foundations in algebraic geometry, it was difficult for me to follow.

Now, years later, many of his ideas are finally falling into place. They have helped me build my foundations and develop a deeper intuition for stacks. While writing this thesis, I often found myself recalling our conversations about homotopy limits and colimits. Concepts I initially only understood in the setting of infinity categories. Having developed this perspective further, the chapter on descent now feels like a concrete application of those ideas.

In some sense, this thesis is my final homage to him.

Tim was much more than a brilliant mathematician. He was a fellow student, a tutor, and one of my closest friends. Many of my intuitions about infinity categories and the foundations of higher homotopy theory were shaped in discussions with him. His enthusiasm for “general abstract nonsense” was infectious, and it is something I have carried with me ever since and hope to carry forward.

In this way, I would like to express my deepest gratitude for his lasting impact on my academic path.

Rest in peace.
